Business Architecture Scenario

Using blueprints for strategy impact analysis

See how a strategic decision can be traced through the business before investment begins.

The strategy

Launch a digital self-service channel

A financial services company wants customers to apply for products, upload documents and track applications online. Its goals are to shorten turnaround times, reduce service costs and reach customers beyond its branch network.

The blueprints

Trace the affected business elements

The strategy is overlaid onto connected architecture views:

  • Capability blueprint: identifies digital sales, identity verification, customer support, fraud management and analytics as critical capabilities.
  • Value-stream blueprint: shows where digital steps change the journey from customer enquiry through approval and fulfilment.
  • Process blueprint: reveals manual hand-offs, duplicated checks and controls that must be redesigned.
  • Information blueprint: identifies customer, consent and application data that must be captured, shared and protected.
  • Organisation blueprint: clarifies new responsibilities across sales, operations, compliance and technology teams.
The impact

Make informed investment decisions

The analysis shows that a new customer portal alone will not deliver the strategy. The organisation must also strengthen identity verification, integrate customer data, redesign application processing, update controls and prepare support teams.

Leaders can now compare current and required maturity, expose dependencies and sequence initiatives into a realistic roadmap—with clear ownership, cost drivers, risks and outcome measures.

Result: strategy becomes an actionable change portfolio

The connected blueprints provide traceability from the strategic objective to every capability, process, information requirement, role and enabling technology that must change.
