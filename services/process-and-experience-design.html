Connected Experience Design

Linking customer journeys to the business behind them

A customer journey shows what people experience. Process models explain how work is performed. Business architecture blueprints reveal the capabilities, information, roles and technology that make the experience possible.

1. Map the customer journey

We map customer goals, actions, channels, emotions, waiting points and moments that matter across the end-to-end journey. This outside-in view makes friction visible in the customer’s own context.

2. Connect the process model

Each journey stage is linked to the activities, decisions, hand-offs, controls and service levels inside the organisation. This inside-out view shows the operational causes of delays, repeated requests and inconsistent service.

3. Trace the architecture

Business blueprints connect those processes to enabling capabilities, teams, information, policies, applications and partners. The wider view exposes dependencies and confirms what must change to sustain a redesigned experience.

How the combined view improves customer experience

  • Links customer pain points to their operational root causes instead of treating symptoms.
  • Aligns front-stage interactions with the people, processes and systems working behind the scenes.
  • Prioritises improvements by customer value, business impact, effort and dependency.
  • Defines measures that connect customer outcomes to process and capability performance.

One traceable design

The result is a future-state experience blueprint that traces every improved customer interaction to the process, capability, information, ownership and technology changes required to deliver it consistently.
