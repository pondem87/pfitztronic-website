The Right Level of Formality

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. The Right Level of Formality
Dr Tendai Pfidze
2 August 2026

Structure should enable good decisions

There are those who do everything by the book, and then there are those who despise all formality. Neither extreme is reliable. Too much structure slows work and shifts attention toward compliance. Too little structure creates ambiguity, duplicated effort and decisions that cannot be explained later.

The right level of formality is the minimum structure needed to manage the risk and complexity of a decision. It should help people understand what matters, who is accountable and how to move forward.

Match the method to the decision

A reversible experiment involving one team does not require the same governance as a multi-year platform investment affecting customers, regulators and several business units. Formality should increase with cost, risk, dependency, longevity and the difficulty of reversing a choice.

This principle applies to architecture as much as it does to project delivery. A quick sketch may be enough to align a small team. A strategic change with enterprise-wide consequences requires explicit blueprints, agreed principles, impact analysis and a recorded decision.

Useful documentation earns its place

Documents should exist because someone uses them to decide, deliver, operate or learn. A capability map can clarify investment priorities. A process model can expose a failed hand-off. A solution view can help delivery teams understand boundaries and dependencies.

If an artefact has no audience, decision or maintenance owner, it is likely to become shelfware. The answer is not to abandon documentation, but to make each view purposeful, proportionate and current enough for its intended use.

Questions for choosing the right level

  • What decision or action must this work support?
  • Who needs to understand, approve or maintain it?
  • What is the consequence of misunderstanding the change?
  • How many teams, systems and obligations are affected?
  • What is the lightest evidence that will provide sufficient confidence?

Be disciplined without becoming bureaucratic

Good governance creates clarity and timely decisions. It defines boundaries, makes trade-offs visible and records what others will need to know. The goal is not maximum documentation or maximum freedom; it is enough shared structure for people to act confidently and responsibly.
